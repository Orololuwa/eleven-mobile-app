# Eleven — Amendment 01: Tracking Accuracy & Signal Gating

**Status:** Draft v1
**Milestone:** M0
**Amends:** 0.4 (§5, §10), 0.4.2 (§3, §4, §6), 0.5 (§3.3, §3.9)
**Trigger:** Field session, 13 Oct 2026, Ikeja

---

## 1. Why this amendment exists

The first real field session produced numbers that were not merely inaccurate but internally contradictory. This is the field data that 0.4 §10 and 0.4.2 §7 both predicted would be needed to validate their thresholds, and it arrived faster and more decisively than expected.

**Observed, same phone, same 40 minutes, same weather (heavy thunderstorms, Ikeja):**

| Source | Distance | Duration | Implied average |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eleven | 27.69 km | 40:37 | **40.9 km/h** |
| Nike Run Club | 5.94 km | 41:55 | 8.5 km/h (7'04" pace) |
| StepUp (step counter, no GPS) | ~5.63 km | — | — |

Eleven's HUD simultaneously reported a **top speed of 38.3 km/h** while its own distance and duration implied an **average of 40.9 km/h**. Average cannot exceed peak. The figure was not a measurement of anything.

Two independent sources — Nike and a GPS-free pedometer — agree the real distance was ~5.9 km. Eleven over-reported by **4.66x**, inventing 21.75 km.

**The distance method is not at fault.** The local-tangent-plane change (0.5 §3.3) differs from haversine by 0.2% at Lagos latitude, in the direction of *reducing* distance. No distance formula, correct or broken, produces a self-contradictory average. The inflation is summed GPS jitter: each fix lands somewhere in its error cloud, and summing consecutive noisy positions adds phantom movement every sample.

Back-solving 4.66x against a correlated-error model puts the session at roughly 1 Hz with ~22m error, or 2 Hz with ~10m error — in both cases, **fixes sitting at or just inside our 20m acceptance threshold, passing the filter and accumulating straight into the total.**

**What worked:** the session summary's data-quality gate (0.5 §3.9) classified the session `insufficient` — clear signal for 444s of 2437s, an 82% gap against a 25% floor — and correctly refused to show numbers it could not stand behind. That behaviour needs no change.

**What did not:** the live HUD showed 27.69 km accumulating for forty minutes, and only afterwards did the summary disown it.

## 2. Amendment A1 — Split the accuracy threshold

### The problem

0.4 §5 defines a single 20m horizontal-accuracy gate that decides whether a fix is "accepted." That one number is doing two incompatible jobs:

1. **Continuity** — does this fix prove tracking is alive? (drives the 30s GPS-loss auto-pause)
2. **Quality** — is this fix good enough to measure with? (feeds distance, speed, sprints)

These want opposite values. Continuity wants generosity, because falsely declaring signal loss pauses a session that is tracking fine. Quality wants strictness, because a marginal fix poisons every metric derived from it.

At 20m the single threshold managed to be **wrong in both directions at once** in the same session: strict enough to trigger spurious signal-loss pauses, loose enough to let 21.75 km of phantom distance through.

### The change

Replace the single threshold with two:

| Gate | Threshold | Governs |
|---|---|---|
| **Continuity** | ≤ 50 m | Whether tracking is alive. Resets the 30s GPS-loss timer; prevents auto-pause |
| **Quality** | ≤ 10 m | Whether a fix may contribute to distance, speed, sprint detection, or any stored metric |

**Every fix within the continuity threshold is still stored in the trace**, with its `horizontal_accuracy_m` intact. Storage is not gated on quality — only measurement is. This preserves the raw record for later re-analysis (including re-deriving metrics under revised thresholds, which §5 makes likely) and keeps route rendering and heatmaps working through stretches too marginal to measure from.

A fix between 10m and 50m therefore means: *we know where the player is, roughly; we are not pausing; we are not counting it.*

### Knock-on: `gap_seconds` becomes quality-aware, and acceptance leaves the classifier

The split creates a session state 0.5 §3.9 cannot currently express: fixes arriving steadily at 30m accuracy. Continuity holds, so no pause fires and no gap accrues — but nothing feeds the metrics. That session would classify `good` while having no measurable data at all.

**`gap_seconds` must accrue on quality-gate failure, not only on continuity failure.** This is not a redefinition. 0.5 §3.3 already defines gap as time that counts toward duration while contributing zero distance — time we could not measure. An interval covered only by sub-quality fixes is exactly that. Applying the existing definition to the case A1 newly creates closes the hole, and the 5% / 25% gap thresholds keep working with their original meaning.

The 13 Oct session confirms the behaviour: 444s of clear signal in 2437s is an 82% gap, far past the 25% floor, classifying `insufficient` on gap alone — which is what actually happened.

**Acceptance then drops out of the classifier.** This resolves a problem that predates the amendment. 0.5 §3.9 defines acceptance as `accepted_fix_count ÷ active_duration_seconds` — a fix *density*, not a pass rate. The 0.7 / 0.4 cutoffs therefore encode an assumption about sampling rate, not about fix quality, and one metric is carrying three unrelated signals: sampling rate (device-dependent), fix quality (now gap's job), and signal availability (already gap's job).

It also means a device sampling at 0.5 Hz is flagged `insufficient` with perfect fixes — a latent bug today, which the quality gate would have worsened by lowering density further.

Acceptance reverts to its one honest job, detecting inadequate sampling: redefine it rate-aware as `accepted_fix_count ÷ fixes_delivered_within_continuity`, keep recording it, and leave it **non-binding** until real sessions provide a distribution to set a floor from.

**Correction to an earlier draft of this amendment:** it claimed these cutoffs "were calibrated against a 20m gate" and must be re-derived before shipping. That overstates their standing. They appear in 0.5 §3.9 with no empirical basis — reasoned guesses of the same class as the 20m gate beside them. There is nothing to re-derive back to, and no second field session is needed to proceed.

## 3. Amendment A2 — Gate speed on horizontal accuracy; document F3's blindness

### The problem

The 38.3 km/h top speed passed all four filters in 0.4.2 §4, for a player whose true average was 8.5 km/h:

| Filter | Outcome |
|---|---|
| F1 — speed accuracy | Did not reject (not reported, or within threshold) |
| F2 — 40 km/h ceiling | Passed, by 1.7 km/h |
| F3 — position cross-check | **Corroborated the bad reading** |
| F4 — sustained window | Passed; jitter is sustained |

**F3 failed structurally, not by miscalibration.** It compares OS-reported speed against position-delta speed, which assumes position is the trustworthy reference. When position noise is itself the fault, the delta is inflated by the same noise — so the cross-check confirms the garbage instead of catching it. F3 can catch a Doppler glitch against good position data. It is blind, by construction, to correlated position noise.

That left F2 — a hard physical bound, designed as a last-resort sanity check — as the only thing standing between the player and a worse number, holding by 1.7 km/h. A backstop was doing a filter's job.

### The change

**A2.1 — F1 absorbs horizontal accuracy.** F1 currently checks `speed_accuracy_mps` only. It becomes a confidence gate over both fields: a reading is ineligible as a top-speed candidate if `horizontal_accuracy_m` exceeds the A1 quality threshold (10m), *regardless of what F3 concludes*. This is the primary fix — it rejects the 13 Oct reading outright, before F3 is ever consulted.

**A2.2 — F3 becomes two-sided.** F3 currently rejects only when position-delta speed is *far below* the averaged OS speed (the Doppler-glitch signature). Add the symmetric case: reject when delta-speed is far *above* averaged OS speed, which is the signature of a position jump — Doppler says the player is slow, but the coordinates moved a long way. The existing 50% lower gate is joined by an upper gate at 200% (its reciprocal), keeping one tunable ratio rather than two.

**A2.3 — Document the limitation.** 0.4.2 §4 must state plainly that F3 cannot detect correlated position noise, and that F1's horizontal-accuracy gate is what covers that case. Leaving the assumption implicit is what let this ship.

## 4. Amendment A3 — Gate the HUD live

### The problem

The quality gate exists only at summary time. The live HUD has no equivalent, so it displayed 27.69 km with full confidence for forty minutes before the summary disowned it. From the player's side the app asserted a number and then retracted it — worse for trust than showing nothing, because it was shown with no hedge at any point.

### The change

The HUD computes the same data-quality classification as 0.5 §3.9, continuously, over the session so far, and degrades as soon as it would classify the session below `good`:

| Live classification | HUD behaviour |
|---|---|
| `good` | Elapsed time, distance, top speed — as now |
| `estimated` | All three shown, with a visible signal-quality indicator |
| `insufficient` | **Distance and top speed suppressed.** Elapsed time remains, with a clear "poor signal — not tracking accurately" state |

Elapsed time is never suppressed: it comes from the clock, not the GPS, and is trustworthy under any signal conditions.

**The guiding rule: the HUD must never display a number the summary would later refuse to show.** Any future change to §3.9's thresholds applies to both surfaces, or they drift apart again.

**Secondary benefit.** The 13 Oct summary advised *"Start before you walk on. The phone needs a few seconds in open sky to lock on."* That is good advice delivered too late to act on. A live `insufficient` state surfaces the problem while the player can still do something — move into open sky, wait for lock, restart the session.

## 5. What this amendment does **not** fix

**A1–A3 make the app honest about bad data. They do not make bad data good.** These are separate problems and this amendment only closes the first.

A tighter quality threshold reduces inflation but cannot eliminate it. Modelled against the 13 Oct session's true 5.75 km:

| Quality threshold | Measured at 1 Hz | Measured at 2 Hz |
|---|---|---|
| 20 m (current) | 24.85 km (4.32x) | 48.98 km (8.52x) |
| 10 m (A1) | 13.18 km (2.29x) | 24.88 km (4.33x) |
| 5 m | 8.03 km (1.40x) | 13.21 km (2.30x) |
| 3 m | 6.55 km (1.14x) | 8.97 km (1.56x) |

Even a 3m gate leaves 14% inflation at 1 Hz, and a gate that strict would reject most fixes in the conditions this app is built for. **Summing consecutive raw positions inflates distance at every accuracy level; thresholds only change by how much.**

A naive minimum-displacement gate is not the answer and should not be attempted: modelled earlier, a 4m gate cut a *clean* jog's real distance from 5.04 km to 1.90 km while barely denting the noisy case. It destroys real movement faster than phantom movement.

**The actual fix is smoothing the position track before distance accumulation** — a Kalman filter or equivalent, which is what Nike and comparable apps run. That is a substantial piece of work and is deliberately out of scope here. It should be specced next, as Amendment 02 or a standalone, and it should land before distance is presented as a headline number anyone compares.

Until it does, A1–A3 mean the app will correctly *refuse* to show inflated distance rather than displaying it — which is the right failure mode, and a strict improvement, but not a working distance metric in poor conditions.

**Also outstanding, from the same session:** Eleven failed to track while Nike succeeded on the same phone in the same storm. The leading hypothesis is that Nike uses Play Services' fused location provider, blending GPS with WiFi, cell and motion sensors, while Eleven consumes raw GPS fixes. Confirming the client's current provider, and moving to fused location if it is not already, is likely a larger accuracy win than any threshold in this document. The pedometer corroboration (StepUp within 5% using no GPS at all) also suggests step count is worth carrying as an independent cross-check.

## 7. Revised tuning parameters

| Parameter | Was | Now | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Continuity gate | 20 m (shared) | 50 m | Generous by design; false signal-loss is worse than a marginal fix |
| Quality gate | 20 m (shared) | 10 m | Field-indicated; see §5 — necessary, not sufficient |
| F1 fields | `speed_accuracy_mps` | + `horizontal_accuracy_m` ≤ quality gate | Covers F3's structural blind spot |
| F3 gate | reject if delta < 50% of averaged OS speed | reject if delta < 50% **or > 200%** | Upper bound detects position jumps |
| `gap_seconds` basis | continuity failure only | + quality-gate failure | Closes the "steady 30m fixes read as `good`" hole |
| 0.5 gap thresholds | 5% / 25% | **unchanged** | Meaning preserved; no new numbers required |
| 0.5 acceptance | `fixes ÷ active_seconds`, binding at 0.7 / 0.4 | `fixes ÷ fixes_delivered`, **non-binding** | Was a density measure encoding a sampling-rate assumption, not fix quality |

Nothing in this amendment requires a numeric threshold that field data cannot yet support. Gap carries the classification with its existing cutoffs; the one quantity that genuinely needs more sessions — a sampling-rate floor — is isolated in a single non-binding number rather than blocking the change.

Every value here remains provisional. One session in heavy thunderstorms is a sharper signal than the guesses it replaces, but it is still one session, in bad weather, on one device.
