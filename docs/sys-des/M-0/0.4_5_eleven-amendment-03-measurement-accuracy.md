# Eleven — Amendment 03: Measurement Accuracy

**Status:** Phase 1 built (C1, C4, C5, `v3`) — 21 Oct 2026. C2 / C3 (motion sensors) pending; field validation against NRC pending. See §8.
**Milestone:** M0
**Amends:** 0.4 (§5, §7), 0.4.2 (§4, §6), 0.5 (§3.3, §3.9, §5.1)
**Goal:** Distance and speed within a few percent of Nike Run Club on the same phone, in the conditions this app actually runs in.
**Depends on:** Amendment 02 B0/B0.1 and **B2.1 direct GPS subscription** — both shipped. The provenance precondition in §3.4 is satisfied by B2.1.
**Supersedes:** Amendment 02 B4 (step count), which moves here

---

## 1. What is actually wrong

Amendments 01 and 02 made the app honest about bad data and stopped it losing the fix stream. Neither made good data accurate. Measured on device, 14 Oct, outdoors at walking pace with fixes at 3–8 m and 1 Hz:

| Session | Fixes | Old sum | Quality-gated sum | Doppler-integrated | Gated ÷ Doppler |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `8cfcf24f` | 181 | 101 m | 92 m | 61 m | **1.51x** |
| `72b8cea0` | 217 | 128 m | 116 m | 63 m | **1.84x** |

Two things in that table set the whole design.

**The quality gate removed only ~10%.** These fixes were already accurate. Tightening thresholds further cannot fix this — Amendment 01 §5 models that even a 3 m gate leaves 14% inflation, and a gate that strict rejects most fixes in real conditions.

**Doppler-integrated distance was already closest to truth in both sessions.** OS speed is derived from the Doppler shift of the satellite signal, not from differencing positions, so it does not scatter the way position does. Σ(speed × dt) landed at 61 m and 63 m where position summing gave 92 m and 116 m.

**So the central change is not smoothing positions. It is deriving distance from velocity, and using position to correct it.** That inverts the current algorithm, which derives everything from position and treats speed as a readout.

This is also why the app is further from NRC at low speed than at running pace: position scatter is roughly constant in metres while real displacement per second shrinks, so the error ratio grows as the player slows. Football is mostly low speed.

---

## 2. Target and how it is judged

**Target:** within 5% of NRC over a session, in both clean and degraded conditions, with no session reading high.

Err low rather than high. An under-read is a disappointment; an over-read is the 13 October failure, and it destroys trust in every other number the app shows.

**Three references, already demonstrated to agree:**

| Reference | Independent of | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Nike Run Club | nothing — same fix stream (16 Oct dumpsys) | 7.20 km / 12.50 km runs |
| Step counter (StepUp, or NRC cadence) | GPS entirely | 5.63 km vs NRC 5.94 km, 13 Oct; NRC cadence 147/134 spm at 0.92/0.98 m/step |
| Doppler-integrated distance | position noise | 61 m / 63 m above |

## 3. The changes

### 3.0 What a Kalman filter is doing here

The algorithm is only as good as the intuition behind it, so this is written down rather than assumed.

**The problem is two unreliable witnesses.** At any moment there are two answers to "where is the player":

- **Prediction** — from the last known position and velocity. Right over short intervals, drifts over long ones, and wrong whenever the player accelerates or turns.
- **Measurement** — what GPS reports. Doesn't drift, but scatters: the ±3–50 m cloud that has produced every inflation figure in these documents.

Neither is trustworthy alone. The filter's job is to combine them, continuously, weighting each by how much it deserves to be believed right now.

**It tracks an estimate *and* its uncertainty.** This is the part that matters and the part a moving average cannot do. The filter carries a state — `[x, y, vx, vy]` — and alongside it a **covariance**: how wrong that state might be. The covariance is what makes everything else in C1–C5 possible.

**Two steps per fix:**

1. **Predict.** Move the state forward by the elapsed time using current velocity. Uncertainty **grows**, because the model is imperfect — how fast it grows is the *process noise*.
2. **Update.** Compare the prediction against the new measurement. Shift the state toward the measurement by an amount set by the two uncertainties. Uncertainty **shrinks**, because an observation was just made.

**The weighting is the whole trick.** If the prediction is confident and the fix reports 40 m accuracy, the filter barely moves — the fix is noted, not obeyed. If the fix reports 3 m, the filter moves most of the way to it. **This is why accuracy becomes a weight rather than a pass/fail gate:** a 40 m fix still contributes a little information, which is strictly better than either discarding it (v2's 10 m gate, under-reads) or trusting it fully (v1's no gate, 4.66x).

#### Process noise — the one real tuning decision

**What it is.** The prediction step assumes velocity stays constant between fixes. That is false the moment a player accelerates. Process noise is the filter's admission of how false — expressed as **σ_a, the unmodelled acceleration in m/s²** that should be expected between one fix and the next. It is not a fudge factor; it is a physical claim about the sport.

Rough bearings: a player standing or walking is near 0; steady jogging around 0.5; a recreational player changing direction or breaking into a run, 2–4; a hard sprint start from standstill, 3–8 at the peak.

**What it controls, in practice: the filter's memory.** A high σ_a means the prediction is quickly distrusted, so each new fix pulls the estimate hard — short memory, responsive, noisy. A low σ_a means the prediction is trusted, so fixes nudge rather than move it — long memory, smooth, slow.

Simulated at 5 m fixes, 1 Hz, with a sprint start (3 m/s² for 2 s up to 6 m/s at t=5 s):

| σ_a | Steady gain | ≈ memory | Reported speed at t = 7, 8, 9, 10 s *(true 6.0)* | Phantom distance over 60 s standing *(true 0)* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.2 | 0.25 | 4 fixes | 0.90 · 1.41 · 1.91 · 2.35 | 11 m |
| 0.5 | 0.36 | 3 fixes | 1.39 · 2.25 · 3.06 · 3.71 | 20 m |
| 1.0 | 0.47 | 2 fixes | 2.27 · 3.51 · 4.49 · 5.05 | 33 m |
| 2.0 | 0.59 | 2 fixes | 3.63 · 5.06 · 5.80 · 5.82 | 57 m |
| 5.0 | 0.75 | 1 fix | 5.95 · 6.68 · 6.35 · 5.42 | 111 m |
| 10.0 | 0.85 | 1 fix | 8.41 · 6.99 · 5.76 · 4.75 | 196 m |

Read the two right-hand columns together and the trade is stark. **At σ_a = 0.5 the filter is still reporting 3.7 km/h five seconds into a 6 m/s sprint** — it never catches up. **At σ_a = 5 it tracks the sprint but invents 111 m of distance from a player who never moved.** Football demands both: long stationary stretches *and* hard accelerations. Position alone cannot serve them.

**Velocity fusion collapses the trade.** Repeating the sprint test with Doppler speed fused as a second observation (C1):

| σ_a | Reported speed at t = 7, 8, 9, 10 s *(true 6.0)* |
|---|---|
| 0.5 | **5.56** · 5.74 · 5.80 · 5.84 |
| 2.0 | 6.25 · 5.87 · 5.85 · 5.86 |
| 5.0 | 6.28 · 5.86 · 5.85 · 5.86 |

At σ_a = 0.5 the filter reports 5.56 where position alone gave 1.39, and all three settings converge on the right answer. **Velocity fusion makes the filter largely insensitive to this parameter** — because the sprint is observed directly rather than inferred from displacement, so the model no longer has to be loose enough to allow it.

**Which is the real argument for C1's design.** Fusing velocity is not only more accurate (§1); it converts the hardest tuning decision in the amendment into a mild one. **Set σ_a low — 0.5 to 1.0 — and take the smaller phantom-distance penalty**, because the sprint lag that would normally punish a low setting is handled by the Doppler observation instead.

**These are simulations, not measurements.** They establish the shape of the trade and a starting point; §4 tunes against real traces whose truth is bracketed by Doppler and step count. The phantom-distance column also assumes no ZUPT — C2 should remove most of what remains.

**Why the rest of the design falls out of it:**

| Mechanism | What it is, in filter terms |
|---|---|
| **Velocity fusion (C1)** | Doppler speed as a second measurement, updating `vx, vy` directly instead of being inferred from position |
| **ZUPT (C2)** | An extra measurement asserting `v = 0` with very low noise — the filter trusts it almost completely and drift stops |
| **Gap handling (C5)** | Predict without updating. Covariance grows every step; when it crosses a bound, stop claiming to know where the player is |
| **Step bridging (C5)** | A distance measurement with no direction — informs the magnitude, not the position |

**Why not something simpler.** A moving average over positions smooths scatter but also smooths away real sprints, and has no notion of confidence — it cannot tell a tight cluster of 3 m fixes from a loose spread of 40 m ones. A minimum-displacement gate was modelled in Amendment 01 §5 and cut a clean jog from 5.04 km to 1.90 km. The filter is more machinery, but it is the least machinery that can weight a measurement by how much it deserves to be believed.

### 3.1 C1 — Fuse position and velocity in one filter

A constant-velocity Kalman filter over the local tangent plane, reusing `scaleAtLatitude` / `planeMetres` from `geodesic.ts` so the projection is unchanged.

- **State:** `[x, y, vx, vy]` in metres and m/s, relative to the session origin.
- **Process noise:** σ_a ≈ **0.5–1.0 m/s²** as a starting point, deliberately low. §3.0 shows why a low setting is safe once velocity is fused, and costly if it is not.
- **Position update:** each accepted fix, with measurement noise derived from `horizontal_accuracy_m`. A 40 m fix barely moves the estimate; a 3 m fix moves it a lot. **This replaces the quality gate** — accuracy becomes a weight rather than a pass/fail test, which is what makes the two-gate split in Amendment 02 B0.2 unnecessary.
- **Velocity update:** each fix also carries `speed_kmh` and `speed_accuracy_mps`. Feed speed as a **second, independent observation** with its own noise term. This is free — both fields are already stored — and the table in §1 says it is the single most valuable input the app has.

**Distance is the integral of filtered velocity**, not the sum of filtered positions. Position's job becomes correcting velocity drift, not producing distance directly.

Heading comes from the filtered velocity vector. Where the OS supplies `bearing`, it can be a third observation; this is optional and should not be built before C1 is validated.

### 3.2 C2 — Zero-velocity updates when the player is standing still

Football is full of standing. Position scatter during a stoppage is pure phantom distance, and it is the single worst case in the §1 table.

The fix is a **zero-velocity update (ZUPT)** — a standard technique in inertial and GPS navigation. When something other than GPS tells you the player is not moving, you feed the filter an explicit observation that velocity is zero, with very low measurement noise so the filter treats it as near-certain.

Because C1 derives distance by integrating velocity, forcing velocity to zero stops distance accumulating outright for as long as the player is still. It also pulls the position estimate back toward a single point instead of letting it wander around the error cloud — so the filter emerges from a stoppage with a cleaner starting position than it would otherwise have.

Detect stationarity from agreement between:

- OS speed below a walking threshold, sustained;
- step cadence at zero (C3);
- accelerometer variance below a floor.

**Require at least two of the three to agree.** A ZUPT applied while the player is actually moving deletes real distance, which is the failure mode of the naive displacement gate rejected in Amendment 01 §5 — a 4 m gate cut a clean jog from 5.04 km to 1.90 km. Agreement across independent sensors is what makes this safe where a threshold on one signal is not.

### 3.3 C3 — Pedometer: cross-check, then fallback *(was Amendment 02 B4)*

Nothing in the tracking feature reads a motion sensor today. NRC reports cadence; StepUp matched NRC within 5% using no GPS at all.

- **C3.1 — Record steps per segment** as `step_count` on `tracking_segments`. The counter runs anyway.
- **C3.2 — Cross-check.** Compare GPS distance against step-implied distance per session. Unlike every check in 0.4.2 this is **genuinely independent of position noise**, which is exactly F3's documented blind spot. A large divergence flags the session.
- **C3.3 — Learn stride length per player.** On sessions the filter rates clean, derive metres-per-step and keep a running per-player estimate, ideally banded by cadence since stride lengthens as pace rises. NRC's figures — 0.92 m/step at 147 spm, 0.98 at 134 — show the relationship is real and learnable from a handful of sessions.
- **C3.4 — Fallback.** Where GPS supplies nothing usable, estimate distance from steps × calibrated stride and mark it as such. A step-derived figure is clearly labelled and never silently mixed with a GPS-derived one in the same number.

C3.4 is what turns the storm session from "no numbers at all" into "numbers we can stand behind, from a different sensor" — which is the M0 habit-formation bar.

### 3.4 C4 — Provenance *(satisfied by Amendment 02 B2.1)*

The filter's trust model rests on reported accuracy being honest. Android's Wi-Fi fallback positions **report 6–7 m** while being nowhere near that good — the figure is a confidence estimate, not a measure of distance from truth. Fed to a filter they would be weighted *heavily*, because it believes the label. That made provenance a hard precondition for C1 while the app consumed a merged fused stream.

**Amendment 02 B2.1 removed the problem at the source.** The session now subscribes to `GPS_PROVIDER` directly, so Android cannot substitute a network position into the stream — every fix C1 sees is a GNSS fix by construction, and its accuracy label comes from the chip.

What remains for C1 to honour:

- **Fixes from any other path are not covered.** If a fused or network subscription is ever reintroduced — including as a fallback for B2.3.1's GPS-disabled case — mixed provenance returns, and C1 must assign those fixes a noise term reflecting what they are rather than what they claim.
- **`altitude_m` stays worth recording** as a cheap validation that the direct stream is behaving as expected.

### 3.5 C5 — Live and summary, reconciled

Amendment 02 B0.2.2 set the rule this implements:

> Both surfaces run the same computation over the same admitted fixes. The summary may refine the live result using full-session information the live pass could not have had. Any material difference is explained to the player.

- **Live:** the filter runs forward, incrementally, as fixes arrive. `LIVE_QUALITY_FIX_ACCURACY_M` and the two-gate split are **retired** — there is one admitted set (continuity) and one computation.
- **Summary:** the same filter, plus a backward **RTS smoother** over the completed session. Strictly more accurate, because it sees what came after each point.
- **Expected divergence is small and one-directional**, and B0.2.1's explanation line changes from *"live used a looser standard"* to *"the final number had the whole session to work with"* — a far better thing to tell a player.

Three details Amendment 03 must specify rather than leave to implementation.

#### Out-of-order fixes

Batched delivery has not been observed on the test device (`batchSize: 1` throughout), but the plugin already handles both shapes — `KEY_LOCATION_CHANGED` for single fixes, `LocationResult` for batches — and Android may defer updates on any device. Build for it.

**The summary pass is immune.** `getPointsForSession` orders by `recorded_at ASC, id ASC`, so the batch filter always sees correct chronological order however fixes arrived. Nothing to do there.

**Only the live filter needs a policy**, because it is sequential and stateful. It **buffers briefly and emits in timestamp order.** The window must exceed the worst-case arrival skew:

> `W > L_max − L_min − Δ`
> where `L` is delivery latency and `Δ` is the timestamp gap between the two fixes (at least the sampling interval).

Measured from the instrumented log: latency min 36 ms, median 94 ms, max 1389 ms. At 1 Hz that gives `W > 353 ms`.

**`LIVE_REORDER_BUFFER_MS = 1000`** — about 2.5x the measured requirement, and a one-second HUD lag nobody perceives.

**It is a static constant, not an adaptive one.** "Derived from measured latency" means the number has a recorded derivation, not that it is recomputed at runtime. Adapting it live would add state, and a latency spike would silently widen the window and lengthen HUD lag — paying complexity for a failure whose cost is one dropped live fix that the summary counts anyway.

**The window is coupled to sample rate, inversely.** The same latency spread needs `W > 853 ms` at 2 Hz and `W > 1153 ms` at 5 Hz. If Amendment 03's deferred rate increase ever lands, **1 s stops being sufficient**. Recompute from the formula at that point; do not guess.

**Instrument the failure rather than watching for it.** Count fixes discarded for arriving older than the filter's current estimate, and report it per session. A counter that stays at zero says the window is sufficient; one that climbs says retune, and by how much. That turns "revisit based on observation" into a number rather than a habit.

**No window survives true batching**, where Android may defer minutes of fixes. That is acceptable because **the live figure is transient and lossy by design**: a fix the live filter drops is still in the database and still counted in the saved number. Live can lose data; the record cannot.

One dependency: the live filter must dedupe on `recorded_at`, or Amendment 02 B1's backlog replay double-counts into it. B1.1 provides that.

#### Gaps — the 5 s bridge does not survive v3

`GAP_BRIDGE_MAX_SECONDS` is a binary gate on **pairs of position fixes**. v3 does not derive distance from pairs of position fixes, so the rule has nothing to operate on. It is replaced by three mechanisms that the single rule was conflating:

**1. An uncertainty bound, replacing the time bound.** The filter predicts through a gap from last known velocity, with position covariance growing as it goes. That *is* bridging — continuous and honest, rather than a cliff at five seconds. Accumulate distance while covariance stays under a bound; stop when it does not. A 5 s gap at a steady jog is far more trustworthy than a 5 s gap mid-sprint, and a covariance bound expresses that where a fixed time cannot.

**2. ZUPT, which is orthogonal.** ZUPT answers *"are you moving?"*; a gap asks *"where are you?"* Neither covers the other.

**3. Steps, which disambiguate the gap.** During a GPS gap the step counter keeps running:

| Gap with | Means | Response |
|---|---|---|
| **0 steps** | Standing still | ZUPT — zero distance, position held, high confidence |
| **N steps** | Moved N × calibrated stride | Distance from steps; **direction unknown** |

This turns a gap from "we know nothing" into "we know how far, not where," and is the main reason C3 belongs in this amendment rather than the previous one.

**Distance and position must therefore be bridged separately.** Steps bridge distance. **Steps cannot bridge position** — they carry no direction — so the position trace must break at any gap where covariance exceeds the bound, and the heatmap renders nothing across it rather than interpolating. One rule can no longer serve both metrics.

#### `algorithmVersion`

Currently `'v2'`. This is `'v3'`, and every stored metric computed under v2 is superseded. Its entry in the version registry (0.5 §6.2) is written in the same commit as the constant, including the measured error against NRC once C5's validation produces one.

## 4. Validation

**The traces are already on the device.** Sessions `8cfcf24f` and `72b8cea0` have known position-sum, quality-gated and Doppler-integrated figures (§1), and the 13 Oct session should still be there unsynced. The existing vitest replay harness — `toComputationInput` → `computeSessionMetrics` — runs new algorithms over real recorded sessions with no field test at all.

**Tune against replay first, then confirm in the field.** Process noise and the ZUPT thresholds are the parameters that matter; both can be swept offline against traces whose truth is bracketed by Doppler and step count.

**Field protocol:** Eleven + NRC + a step counter, same phone, same run. Repeat in clean conditions and in degraded ones (under cover, built-up area, poor sky). Record Eleven's figure, NRC's, step count, and a `dumpsys` capture. Accept at ≤5% divergence from NRC with no session reading high.

**Ship behind a flag** that can compute both v2 and v3 over the same session and log the difference, so the first real sessions produce a comparison rather than a replacement.

## 5. What this does not solve

**Accuracy is bounded by the fix stream.** A session where the chip never locks will not be rescued by better maths; it is rescued by C3.4's step fallback, and that is an estimate with a label, not a measurement.

**Stride calibration needs sessions to learn from.** C3.3 is cold-start-dependent: a new player's first fallback session uses a population default and will be less accurate. Worth saying so in the UI rather than discovering it in a review.

**Sample rate is unlocked by this amendment, not used by it.** Amendment 02 B2.1 defers a faster request because more samples means more scatter while positions are summed. Once C1 lands the relationship reverses — extra samples become extra evidence. Raising the rate is a follow-on, after C1 is validated at 1 Hz, and it needs its own battery measurement over a full 90 minutes.

## 7. Notes

**The measured data chose this design, not the literature.** A Kalman filter over positions was the obvious answer after 13 October and would have helped. But the 14 Oct measurements show Doppler-integrated distance already beating the gated position sum by 1.5–1.8x, which says the dominant error is in *how distance is derived*, not in how positions are cleaned. Fusing velocity as a first-class observation is the change; smoothing positions is what makes it robust.

**This is the first amendment where the fix is bigger than the bug report.** Amendments 01 and 02 were corrections to things that were wrong. This one builds something that was never there, so it should be sequenced and validated like a feature — flagged, replayed against stored traces, measured against three independent references — rather than shipped as a fix.

| `summary/run-computation.ts` | Stores v3; with `METRICS_COMPARISON_ENABLED` also computes v2, v3-live and the Doppler integral into `metrics_comparisons` and logs `[metrics] v3 vs v2` |

### Decisions that differ from, or add to, §3

- **Live is a recomputed forward pass, not an incremental filter.** The live snapshot already recomputes from SQLite each second; points come back ordered by `recorded_at`, so the live pass sees time order by construction. `LIVE_REORDER_BUFFER_MS` and the discard counter are therefore not needed. The cost is a full forward pass per snapshot (4×4 maths per fix; negligible at 90 min × 1 Hz).
- **Fused fixes are back** (the Android foreground watch is a fallback when direct GPS is quiet), so the §3.4 caveat applies: fused fixes get a 10 m position floor and a 2 m/s speed floor. Play Services reports a flat 1.5 m/s speed accuracy indoors whatever the fix (21 Oct capture).
- **Doppler has no heading.** The speed observation is applied along the filter's heading once it is confident in it (speed ≥ 1.2 × its spread). Below that, only a reading ≤ 0.3 m/s is used, as v = (0, 0) — the chip pins speed to exactly 0 when static.
- **Maneuver detection.** When Doppler misses the predicted speed by > 3σ, that interval's prediction is widened. Without it the smoother back-dated a sprint start by a full second (+28 % on the ramp in replay).
- **Outlier gate with lock-out guard.** Position fixes beyond Mahalanobis² 13.8 are rejected (F3's position cross-check, subsumed); after 3 consecutive rejections the next fix is accepted and the filter re-anchors. An early wrong heading otherwise rejected every true fix for the rest of the run.
- **Gap rule.** An interval counts while the *predicted* position σ at its end is ≤ 12 m; a silence > 30 s or a manual pause starts a fresh run. A stream of only ~30 m fixes therefore measures nothing, live or summary — v2's two-gate split showed it live and dropped it in the summary.
